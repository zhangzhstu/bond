What the kernel
actually measures

Deep-kernel Gaussian processes fit a neural feature map and a kernel together, from twenty molecules. Nobody manages the geometry of the space in between. We show what that costs, and what a bounded feature map does and does not fix.

1The thing nobody looks at

A Gaussian process decides everything it decides about a new assay through the pairwise distances between the mapped support molecules. The features and the length‑scale enter the kernel only as a ratio, ‖zi−zj‖ / ℓ. So if the map rescales, the kernel cannot tell the difference between a better representation and a bigger one.

With twenty support points that is not a hypothetical. The same molecules, run through the same encoder at three different scales, give you a kernel that is rank one, a kernel that is the identity, and a kernel that is useful — and only the middle one lets you estimate ℓ at all.

2Two things go wrong

first

The map is unbounded

A linear or ReLU head can grow without limit. The marginal likelihood cannot penalise it, because growing the features and growing ℓ look identical to the kernel. As the scale runs away the length‑scale stops being identifiable, curvature along it goes to zero, and the hypergradient the outer loop needs becomes ill‑posed.

second

The two views are not commensurable

A 300‑dimensional learned graph embedding and a 2048‑bit fingerprint do not contribute comparably to Euclidean distance. We measured it: the fingerprint block carries 83–92% of the squared pairwise distance going into the map. The topology the encoder learned barely reaches the kernel.

3The head

BOND — Bounded Output Neural Dendrite. Each output coordinate is a sum of M saturating responses, each weighted by a coefficient that is itself range‑limited:

Because ς(·) ∈ (0,1) and tanh(w) ∈ (−1,1), every coordinate stays inside (−M, M) whatever the encoder does. The range is fixed by the parameterisation, not restored afterwards by a normalisation layer — so boundedness is structural rather than learned, and the diameter of the whole feature space is bounded by a constant that does not depend on the data.

Nothing else changes. Same encoder, same Gaussian process, same bi‑level meta‑learning loop. Only the map between them.

5The result that argues against us

We went looking for evidence that the bound is what produces the accuracy. We did not find it, and the paper says so.

Measured at initialisation and again after 2000 epochs, the three heads that train to completion have raw feature diameters four orders of magnitude apart. Divide each distance by its own median — which is exactly what the median‑heuristic length‑scale initialisation does — and they converge: coefficients of variation of 0.30, 0.30 and 0.29. Almost all of the scale variation the bound was introduced to control is absorbed before it reaches the kernel.

The ablation points the same way. The arm that aborts meta‑training is the bounded single‑branch head; the unbounded branched head completes every run. Across the arms it is branch structure, not boundedness, that tracks whether the inner solve stays well posed.

So: the accuracy is robust to the feature map rather than produced by it. That is a weaker claim than the one we set out to make, and it is the one the measurements support.
